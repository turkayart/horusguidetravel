About

Discover Karnak Temple

Karnak Temple is the largest religious building ever constructed. This vast complex of temples, pylons, and obelisks was built over 2,000 years by more than 30 pharaohs, each adding their own contributions. The Great Hypostyle Hall alone contains 134 massive columns, some standing 23 meters tall.

Construction began during the Middle Kingdom around 2000 BC and continued through the Ptolemaic period. Karnak was the most important temple complex in Egypt, dedicated primarily to Amun-Ra, the king of the gods. The site covers over 200 acres — large enough to contain ten average European cathedrals. The Avenue of Sphinxes, recently restored, connects Karnak to Luxor Temple 2.7 kilometers away.

Historical Timeline

c. 2000 BC
Senusret I begins the first construction at Karnak during the Middle Kingdom.
c. 1479 BC
Hatshepsut adds her red chapel and famous obelisks to the complex.
c. 1279 BC
Ramesses II completes the Great Hypostyle Hall with its 134 massive columns.
c. 380 BC
Nectanebo I builds the last major Egyptian addition to the temple.
1979
Karnak is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site along with ancient Thebes.
2021
The Avenue of Sphinxes between Karnak and Luxor Temple is reopened to the public.

Essential Info

Visitor Tips

Everything you need to know for a perfect visit.

🌅

Go Early

Arrive at 6 AM opening to photograph the Hypostyle Hall bathed in golden sunrise light.

🎫

Luxor Pass

If visiting multiple temples, the Luxor Pass offers unlimited access and saves money.

🏛️

Plan 3 Hours

The complex is massive — budget at least 2-3 hours to see the main highlights.

🌙

Night Show

The Sound & Light Show runs nightly and is one of Egypt's best evening experiences.

📖

Hire a Guide

A local guide brings the hieroglyphics and history to life — highly recommended.

💧

Stay Hydrated

Limited shade inside the complex. Bring water and wear a hat.

Best Time to Visit

November to February for pleasant weather. Early morning visits are best year-round.
