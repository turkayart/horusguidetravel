About

Discover Luxor Temple

Unlike other Egyptian temples dedicated to gods, Luxor Temple was built to celebrate the rejuvenation of kingship. Situated right on the banks of the Nile in the heart of modern Luxor, the temple is uniquely stunning when illuminated at night, its massive columns and statues glowing golden against the dark sky.

Largely built by Amenhotep III and Ramesses II during the New Kingdom, Luxor Temple served as the site of the annual Opet Festival, when statues of Amun, Mut, and Khonsu were paraded from Karnak to Luxor Temple. Alexander the Great later added a shrine, and a 14th-century mosque was built atop its ruins. The Avenue of Sphinxes that connects it to Karnak was reopened in 2021 after decades of excavation.

Historical Timeline

c. 1400 BC
Amenhotep III begins construction of the temple's inner sanctum and colonnade.
c. 1279 BC
Ramesses II adds the massive pylon entrance, colossal statues, and obelisks.
332 BC
Alexander the Great builds a chapel inside the temple's inner sanctum.
14th century
Abu al-Haggag Mosque is built atop the buried temple courtyard.
1884
Gaston Maspero begins excavating the temple from centuries of accumulated sediment.
2021
The Avenue of Sphinxes to Karnak Temple is reopened in a grand ceremony.

Visitor Tips

Everything you need to know for a perfect visit.

🌙

Visit at Night

The illuminated temple is one of Egypt's most magical experiences. Open until 9 PM.

🏛️

Combine with Karnak

Do Karnak in the morning and Luxor Temple in the evening for the perfect day.

🕌

Mosque Note

The Abu al-Haggag Mosque is still active — be respectful during prayer times.

📷

The courtyard of Ramesses II is most photogenic just after sunset with the lights on.

Avenue Walk

Walk the Avenue of Sphinxes in the evening when the sphinx statues are illuminated.
